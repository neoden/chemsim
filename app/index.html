About the model

The electrons are computed with the GFN2-xTB method right in the browser. What you see are the valence electrons: the inner shells are not part of this method.

What the model cannot do

  • The nuclei are classical. There is no zero-point motion, and at low temperatures the amplitudes are too small.
  • Dissociation at realistic temperatures is too rare to wait for. Heating to thousands of kelvin is a way to speed the event up.
  • The electrostatic potential is that of the valence electrons and the atomic cores, with the electron cloud replaced by point charges, dipoles and quadrupoles on the nuclei and on the bonds. On the coloured surface that is within some 0.05 V of the potential of the cloud itself. The cloud comes from a minimal set of functions, so the numbers are approximate; where the surface is red and where it is blue can be trusted.
  • The method is fitted to geometries, frequencies and intermolecular interactions. Reaction energies and barrier heights are approximate.
  • Light in a model like this can only be a classical wave. There is no spontaneous emission.

Coordinates (XYZ)

One atom per line: element, then x, y, z in angstrom. Copy the text out, or put another geometry in and replace the molecule with it.

Element

The element the Draw tool puts in. Every element here has parameters in GFN2-xTB; the ones that have no usual number of bonds (the metals, the noble gases) arrive without hydrogens, and their bonds are drawn by hand.

Find or build a molecule

Type the name of a compound to take it from PubChem, or its formula in SMILES to build it right here. A search sends what you typed to PubChem, a database of the U.S. National Library of Medicine; nothing else leaves this page.
